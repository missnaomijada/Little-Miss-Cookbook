Marinade School

Longer is not automatically better. Salt, acid, enzymes, dairy, fat, sugar and aromatics behave differently, and the cut matters.

ComponentWhat it contributesWatch for
Salt / brineSeasoning and, depending on method, moisture retention.Concentration and time matter. Do not stack salty ingredients blindly.
AcidBrightness and surface protein changes.Very acidic marinades can make delicate proteins unpleasant if left too long.
DairyFlavour, coating and gentle tenderising effects in suitable dishes.Keep refrigerated and use only where it belongs to the cuisine/recipe.
Enzyme-rich ingredientsCan tenderise proteins.Some fruits can soften surfaces aggressively, so timing needs tighter control.
OilCarries fat-soluble aromas and coats the surface.Oil is not a substitute for seasoning.
Sugar / honeySweetness, glaze and browning.Can burn at high heat before the centre is cooked.
Herbs, spices & aromaticsAroma and identity.Fresh garlic/herbs and dried spices behave differently under long/high heat.
Every marinated recipe will show: minimum useful time, ideal window, maximum recommended time where relevant, refrigeration/storage, why that timing works, what a shorter marinade can still achieve, and whether overnight actually helps. Thick meat is not magically seasoned to the centre just because it sat in liquid longer.

Food Science 🧪

Roux School

Equal-ish flour and fat is the starting pattern. White roux keeps more thickening power; longer-cooked blond/brown roux develops nuttier flavour while gradually losing thickening strength.

Browning

Maillard: amino acids + reducing sugars create savoury roasted complexity. Caramelisation: sugars break down and transform under heat. They overlap in a kitchen, but they are not the same process.

Emulsions

Oil and water normally separate. Emulsifiers and technique can hold tiny droplets together. Heat, ratios and agitation explain why some sauces split.

Flavour Balance

Think sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami, fat, heat, aroma and texture. “Flat” does not automatically mean “add salt.” Diagnose first.

Luxury & Depth

Browned butter, fond, reductions, stock, slow onions, mushrooms, aged cheese, citrus zest, herbs, spices and deliberate texture contrast create depth.

Why Food Feels Heavy

Perceived richness can rise with concentrated fat, dense starch, sugar, cream sauces and portion size. Acidity, herbs, vegetables, bitterness and crunch can make a rich plate feel more balanced.

Sauce & Roux School 🥄

White → blond → brown roux

Cook flour in fat. Pale roux keeps more thickening power; longer cooking develops toastier flavour while reducing thickening strength.

Béchamel

Build a pale roux, gradually whisk in milk, then simmer gently until the starch is cooked and the sauce is smooth. Season once the structure is right.

Cheese sauce

Start with a smooth base, lower the heat, then add suitable cheese gradually. Boiling after cheese is added can tighten proteins and make the sauce grainy or oily.

Gravy

Use fond and roasting juices for depth, manage excess fat, add stock gradually and reduce to the concentration you want.

Velouté

A pale roux thickened with light stock rather than milk. It teaches how changing the liquid changes an otherwise similar sauce structure.

Other thickeners

Reduction, starch slurry, beurre manié, eggs, purées and emulsification create different textures. Pick the method that belongs to the dish.

Save My Food 😭

Diagnosis → why → safest correction → what not to do → next-time prevention.

🌶️ Too spicy

Rescue

First identify the heat. For chilli/capsaicin, increase the unspiced base or total volume. Dairy/fat can soften perceived chilli heat where it belongs in the dish. Starchy accompaniments can rebalance the meal. Sweetness or acid may balance certain dishes, but neither is a universal eraser.

🧂 Too salty

Add more unsalted base/volume when possible. A potato is not a magic salt vacuum.

🍋 Too sour

Dilute where possible, or balance with richness and sometimes a small amount of sweetness, depending on the dish.

🥣 Too watery

Reduce uncovered if the dish tolerates it, remove excess liquid, or use the appropriate thickening method rather than throwing flour straight in.

🧀 Grainy cheese sauce

Lower the heat. Avoid boiling after cheese goes in. Add suitable cheese gradually. Prevention beats trying to force a broken protein network smooth.

🔥 Burnt bottom

Immediately transfer the unaffected food to a clean pan. Do not scrape the burnt layer into the good food.

🍚 Mushy rice

Stop cooking and vent steam. You cannot truly uncook it. Record liquid ratio, heat and timing for the next attempt.

🍗 Dry meat

Lost moisture cannot be magically restored. Improve serving with sauce or jus; next time control internal temperature and resting.

🍯 Too sweet

Add unsweetened base where possible. Acid, bitterness or salt may rebalance some dishes, but none universally cancels sugar.

😶 Bland / flat

Check salt, acid, umami, fat, heat, aroma and texture one at a time. Taste after each small correction.

🫗 Greasy / split

Decide whether you have excess fat or a broken emulsion. Skim excess fat; for an emulsion, stop aggressive heat and restore structure.

🥚 Curdled custard

Remove from heat immediately. Slight curdling may sometimes improve with rapid cooling and straining/blending; badly scrambled proteins cannot be fully reversed.

🍫 Seized chocolate

If extra liquid suits the final use, controlled warm liquid can sometimes turn seized chocolate into a smooth sauce or ganache. It may no longer suit tempering.

🍬 Crystallised caramel

Prevention matters: keep pan sides clean and avoid unnecessary agitation once boiling. Precision work may require remaking a badly crystallised batch.

Little Miss rule: Don’t just correct the number. Correct the balance without destroying the identity of the dish.

Plating & Texture School 🎨

Plate architecture

Use height, colour, negative space, shape, deliberate sauce placement, contrasting textures and clean plate edges. Garnish should belong to the food.

Texture contrast

Crisp + soft, juicy + crunchy, rich + bright. Texture is part of flavour perception, not decoration.

Roast potatoes

Target a deeply crisp exterior and fluffy interior. Surface roughness, hot fat, spacing and moisture management all matter.

Pizza

Browned underside, crisp edges, airy-chewy crust and molten cheese. Judge the base, rim and top separately.

Rice

For fluffy styles, look for distinct grains rather than a wet mass. The exact target remains recipe-specific.

Crumble + custard

Keep some crisp craggy topping exposed beside soft fruit and silky hot custard so the contrast survives plating.
